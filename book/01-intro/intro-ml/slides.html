Two classic definitions

Definition 1 (Mitchell, 1997)

A computer program is said to learn from experience E with respect to some class of tasks T and performance measure P if its performance at tasks in T, as measured by P, improves with experience E.

In engineering terms: we collect data, build a statistical model from it, and use that model to solve the problem.

Arthur Samuel

IBM researcher. His checkers program improved by playing against itself; his 1959 paper popularized the term machine learning. He later worked on TeX at Stanford.

About the quote

The sentence circulates in this form, but it does not appear verbatim in Samuel's paper. The idea does.

Part 2

The pieces of a model

Data, features and models: they appear in every method in the course.

Data is a table

Each row is an email and each column is something we measured about it.

Example: a row; here, an email described by 4 numbers.

Feature: a column, such as the percentage of capital letters.

Label: the known answer, spam or not spam. It is what the model must learn to predict.

Figure 6:Six of the 400 emails in the example, with four features and their label.

Everything becomes numbers

Figure 7:Three encodings. (a) An image is flattened row by row. (b) A text is summarized by the count of each vocabulary word. (c) A category is encoded with a 1 in its position. A color image is a tensor, an array with three axes: height, width and channel.

A model is a function

input → model → prediction

  • Model family: all the possible functions; in the figure, all straight lines.
  • Parameters: the numbers fitted from the data, such as the slope of the line.
  • Hyperparameters: set before training, such as the degree of a curve.

Part 4

Generalization

What matters is performance on data the model has not seen.

Evaluate on new data

The model learns from the blue group. The orange group is kept aside, like an exam with questions it has never seen.

Warning

Evaluating on the training data rewards memorizing, not learning.

Assumption

Training and test data are alike: they come from the same kind of situation.

Usual splits

80/20 or 70/30. With little data, cross-validation is used (chapter 2).

Figure 14:20 examples shuffled at random and split into training (blue) and test (orange).

The right amount of complexity

High bias

The model is too rigid for the shape of the data. It fails on both training and test.

High variance

The model changes a lot with the particular sample and memorizes the noise. Training goes well and test goes badly.

Figure 16:Error by degree for the models in Figure 15.

Every model makes assumptions

Figure 17:Two models pass through every point, but predict very different values at a new point. The data alone cannot decide which is better.

Key idea

No method is best for every problem. Each one works well when its assumptions match the data.

That preference is called inductive bias. Some examples:

  • k-NN assumes that neighbors are alike.
  • Linear regression assumes a linear relationship.
  • Convolutional networks assume a pattern means the same thing anywhere in the image.

ONNX

An open format for moving a trained model between platforms without the training code.

Distillation

Training a small model to imitate the outputs of a large one, so it is cheaper to run.

The risks are in the data

Biased data

If the data underrepresents a group, the model inherits that bias and fails more often for that group.

Data leakage

Test information that slips into training inflates the metrics without improving the model.

Distribution shift

The world changes after deployment, as in Figure 3. That is why models are monitored.

Privacy

Personal data is used with consent and kept safe; a model can leak its training data.

Key ideas

Remember
  • A program learns if it gets better at a task with experience.
  • A model is a function that turns data into predictions.
  • Training means adjusting the model to reduce its error.
  • The type of learning depends on the information available.
  • A model is judged on data it has not seen.
